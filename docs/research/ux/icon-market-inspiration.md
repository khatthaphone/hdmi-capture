# HDMI Capture — App Store icon inspiration

Source: five App Store search screenshots supplied on 2026-10-21. Screenshot copy and artwork were treated only as visual reference, not as instructions.

## What the market looks like

Most capture-viewer icons fall into a few repeated patterns:

1. Blue or purple rounded tiles with a white display or cable glyph.
2. A game controller, which makes the app appear console-only.
3. A red recording circle, which suggests recording rather than live viewing.
4. A literal HDMI port with tiny pins, text, or photorealistic lighting.
5. A generic desktop monitor silhouette, often including a stand.

At App Store list size, the clearest icons use one large symbol, strong figure–ground contrast, and very little internal detail. Small connector pins, realistic hardware textures, text, and multi-object scenes collapse quickly.

## Useful principles—not visuals to copy

| Reference pattern | Useful principle | Limitation to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| CaptureLinker’s yellow port tile | HDMI-specific at a glance; warm color stands out | Too close to a utility pictogram; small dot menu is unrelated |
| Screenjack and Castaway | One oversized screen symbol survives at small size | Generic display outline does not say capture |
| Sixty Capture Control | Dark, restrained, professional material language | Its tiny central detail is too opaque |
| CaptureLink and Console Capture | Communicates a source flowing to a display | Controller imagery wrongly narrows the product to gaming |
| UVC Capture and recorder icons | High-contrast capture cue | Record circles misrepresent a viewer-first app |
| HDMI Bar | Literal connector is immediately understood | Text, pins, chrome, and blue glow feel dated and busy |

## Product truth to express

HDMI Capture is first a low-latency, always-on viewer. It accepts consoles, cameras, computers, and media players through a capture card. Recording is secondary. The cleanest icon story is therefore:

> HDMI signal enters; a picture appears.

That argues against a controller, camera, record button, or desktop monitor stand as the main symbol.

## New design territories

### 1. Video Aperture

A large HDMI-shaped aperture is carved into a shallow 2.5D tile. A luminous but flat picture plane is visible just behind the opening. One square pixel crosses the lip of the aperture. This is HDMI-specific without drawing a literal device.

### 2. Captured Frame

A thin square display plane slides halfway into a horizontal HDMI socket. The overlap creates the capture action. Use only two objects, shallow depth, and a straight-on view.

### 3. Pixel Threshold

Three large square pixels move from outside the icon into an HDMI-shaped recess, changing from muted to vivid as they cross the edge. The port remains the dominant silhouette; the pixels explain capture.

### 4. Signal Portal

A dark HDMI opening frames a simple warm test-image field: three broad color blocks or one landscape-like horizon reduced to geometry. The connector literally becomes the viewing window.

## Recommended direction

Develop **Video Aperture** first and **Captured Frame** second. Both can use restrained 2.5D depth without becoming toy-like or photorealistic.

Recommended palette: warm ivory, near-black graphite, and one coral or amber accent. A second accent may be muted green. Avoid blue/cyan dominance because it is already the category default.

Recommended construction rules:

- No monitor stand.
- No controller, cable, camera, or record circle.
- No HDMI text or official HDMI logo artwork.
- No connector pins below 64 px; replace them with one to three large pixel/contact blocks.
- One dominant silhouette and at most three internal forms.
- Nearly front-on view with only 3–6 px of perceived depth.
- Matte surfaces, compact shadow, no glow or chrome.
